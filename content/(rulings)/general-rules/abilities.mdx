## Can triggered abilities trigger on an opponent's turn without Reaction? [#triggered-ability-timing]

Yes.
A triggered ability can trigger when its condition is met on any player's turn, unless a limit such as "once each turn" has already been reached.<Rule number="383.3.c" /><Rule number="383.3.e" /><Rule number="383.3.e.1" />

When it triggers, the game adds the ability to the chain as a pending item, meaning its choices and costs still need to be completed before it is ready to resolve.<Rule number="400" /><Rule number="401.1" /><Rule number="402.2" /><Rule number="404.1" />
That preparation is called finalization.
The <Reaction /> keyword is not required because satisfying the trigger condition adds the ability automatically rather than letting its controller choose to activate it.<Rule number="383.3" />

The pending ability may leave the chain without finalizing if its controller declines an initial "you may," cannot make its required legal choices, or declines to pay a required trigger cost.<Rule number="383.3.a.2" /><Rule number="402.4" /><Rule number="404.2" />

<Callout type="idea" title="Example">
	<Card name="Blade Dancer" /> triggers when you choose a friendly unit.
	This can happen on your opponent's turn, for example when you play a <Reaction /> spell that targets one of your own units.
</Callout>

## Can I react to triggered and activated abilities? [#ability-reactions]

Yes, except for abilities that <Add /> resources.

Triggered and activated abilities normally become finalized chain items, meaning they are ready to resolve, and other players then have an opportunity to play reactions.<Rule number="377.3" /><Rule number="383.3" /><Rule number="406.2" /><Rule number="406.4" />
An ability that <Add />s resources instead resolves immediately when finalized, before players receive priority to react.<Rule number="337.2" /><Rule number="429.2" /><Rule number="429.2.a" />

## Does removing a triggered ability's source stop that ability? [#source-removal]

Usually no.
A triggered ability on the chain is separate from its source, so removing the source does not remove the ability.<Rule number="355.9.c" /><Rule number="383.2.a.1" />

Removing the source can still prevent an instruction from working when that instruction checks information from the source as it executes.
Words such as "here" or "my" often act as source referents and require the ability to check the source at that time.<Rule number="359.3.f.1" /><Rule number="359.3.f.2" />
If the source is no longer on the board, information such as its location is unavailable, and any calculation based on that information or instruction that becomes impossible is ignored.<Rule number="359.3.e.6" /><Rule number="359.3.e.12" />
By contrast, information referenced from a trigger condition is recorded when that condition is met and can remain available after the source leaves.<Rule number="359.3.f.3" />

<Callout type="idea" title="Examples">
	If an opponent responds to <Card name="Ashe, Focused" />'s play effect with <Card name="Star-Crossed" /> and returns Ashe to her owner's hand, her ability still resolves.
	Nothing in its instructions requires Ashe to remain on the board.

	If Star-Crossed returns <Card name="Vi, Peacekeeper" /> before her attack trigger resolves, Vi cannot <Stun /> an enemy unit "here."
	Because Vi is no longer on the board, she has no location for "here" to reference, so that instruction is ignored.

	The same applies to <Card name="Azir, Sovereign" />: if Azir leaves the board before his attack trigger resolves, there is no "this battlefield" to which his token units can move.
</Callout>

## When does the game check an "if" condition in a triggered ability? [#trigger-time-conditions]

An "if" condition immediately following the trigger condition is checked when the triggering event occurs.
An "if" condition appearing later in the effect is checked when the ability resolves.

The trigger condition begins with "When," "At," or "The [Nth] time" and includes the event and any qualifiers attached to it.<Rule number="383.2.a" />
An additional "if" statement immediately following that condition is part of the trigger condition, so every requirement in it must be true for the ability to be added to the chain.<Rule number="383.2.a.1" /><Rule number="383.2.c" />
The remaining instructions form the ability's effect and execute when the ability resolves.<Rule number="383.2.b" /><Rule number="406.5" />

<Callout type="idea" title="Example">
	<Card name="Gutter Palace" /> reads "At the start of your Beginning Phase, if you have exactly 4 cards in hand and exactly 4 units at battlefields, you win the game."
	The "if" clause immediately follows the trigger condition, so it is part of that condition and checked when the triggering event occurs.
	The ability is added to the chain only if both requirements are already true at the start of your Beginning Phase.
</Callout>

<Callout type="idea" title="Example">
	<Card name="Loose Cannon" /> reads "At start of your Beginning Phase, draw 1 if you have one or fewer cards in your hand."
	Because the "if" clause follows the instruction "draw 1," the ability is added to the chain every Beginning Phase and checks your hand size only when it resolves.<Rule number="383.2.a.1" /><Rule number="383.2.b" /><Rule number="406.5" />
</Callout>

## When do I choose whether to use a triggered ability that says "you may"? [#optional-trigger-decision]

If "you may" begins the effect, you decide before players can react.
If it appears later, you decide when that instruction resolves.

The first decision happens while the pending ability is being finalized, meaning it is still being prepared for resolution.<Rule number="383.3.a" /><Rule number="402.1" /><Rule number="406.4" />
If you decline, the pending ability is removed from the chain and considered not to have triggered.<Rule number="383.3.a.2" /><Rule number="402.1.a" />
If you accept, you make all choices required for finalization and pay any costs incurred during finalization before the ability can finalize.<Rule number="402.2" /><Rule number="404" />

If "you may" appears later in the effect, you decide whether to perform that instruction when the ability resolves.<Rule number="383.3.a.3" />

## When do I pay a cost in a triggered ability that says "you may"? [#costs-within-instructions]

For a triggered ability written as "[do X] to [do Y]," you pay [do X] before players can react if that cost begins the effect or immediately follows an initial "you may."
If that kind of cost appears later, you pay it when the instruction resolves.<Rule number="383.3.b" /><Rule number="740.4.a.2" /><Rule number="740.4.a.2.a" />

The rules call [do X] a cost within instructions.
When it is paid up front, it becomes the ability's base cost and must be paid before players can react.<Rule number="383.3.b" /><Rule number="383.3.b.1" />
If you decline to pay, the ability is removed from the chain.<Rule number="404.2" />

<Callout type="idea" title="Example">
	<Card name="Vayne, Hunter" />'s ability reads "When I conquer, you may pay <Energy value={1} /> to return me to my owner's hand."
	Because the payment immediately follows "you may," you decide whether to pay <Energy value={1} /> before opponents can react.
	If you pay, opponents can react before the ability returns Vayne to your hand.
</Callout>

## Can triggered abilities trigger on an opponent's turn without Reaction? [#triggered-ability-timing]

Yes.
A triggered ability is put on the chain whenever its condition is met, regardless of whose turn it is, so it does not need <Reaction />.<Rule number="383.3" /><Rule number="383.3.c" />

It first enters as a pending item: it has triggered but must still complete the required choices and costs before it finalizes and can resolve.<Rule number="400" /><Rule number="400.1" />
If its controller declines an initial "you may," that pending ability is removed from the chain and considered not to have triggered.<Rule number="383.3.a.2" />

<Callout type="idea" title="Example">
	<Card name="Blade Dancer" /> triggers whenever you choose a friendly unit.
	This can happen on an opponent's turn when you play a <Reaction /> spell that chooses one of your units.
</Callout>

## Can I react to triggered and activated abilities? [#ability-reactions]

Yes, except for triggered and activated abilities that use <Add />.

Triggered and activated abilities use the chain and become finalized chain items.<Rule number="377.3" /><Rule number="383.3" /><Rule number="406.2" />
Once no pending items remain, you have an opportunity to play Reactions before those abilities resolve.<Rule number="337.4" /><Rule number="406.4" />

An ability that adds resources instead resolves immediately after finalization without passing priority.<Rule number="429.2" /><Rule number="429.2.a" />

## Does removing a triggered ability's source stop that ability? [#source-removal]

No.
A triggered ability finalizes before opponents can react to it.<Rule number="406.2" /><Rule number="406.4" />
Removing its source is not a counter instruction, so the ability remains on the chain and executes like a spell.<Rule number="425.1" /><Rule number="425.2.a" /><Rule number="406.5" />

An instruction can still do nothing if it needs current information from the missing source.
Source referents such as "here," "my," or "this battlefield" are normally checked when that instruction executes.<Rule number="359.3.f.1" /><Rule number="359.3.f.2" />
Information referenced from a triggered ability's trigger condition is instead recorded when the condition is fulfilled and remains available if the source later moves or leaves.<Rule number="359.3.f.3" />
If an instruction instead needs the source's current location and the source is no longer on the board, that information is unavailable, so the instruction cannot execute.<Rule number="359.3.e.12" />

For example, if an opponent responds to <Card name="Ashe, Focused" />'s play effect with <Card name="Star-Crossed" /> and returns Ashe to her owner's hand, her ability still resolves because none of its instructions needs Ashe to remain on the board.
By contrast, if Star-Crossed returns <Card name="Vi, Peacekeeper" /> before her attack trigger resolves, Vi cannot <Stun /> an enemy unit "here" because she no longer has a location for "here" to reference.

## When does the game check an "if" condition in a triggered ability? [#trigger-time-conditions]

An "if" condition immediately after the trigger condition is checked after the triggering event, while one after an effect instruction is checked during resolution.

- **Immediately after the trigger condition:** A trigger condition begins with "When," "At," or "The [Nth] time," and an additional conditional statement immediately following it is part of that condition.<Rule number="383.2.a" /><Rule number="383.2.a.1" />
  The game checks those requirements after the triggering event has been processed.<Rule number="383.2.c" />
  If they are not satisfied then, the ability is not placed on the chain.<Rule number="383.3" />
  For example, <Card name="Gutter Palace" /> checks the hand and battlefield quantities in its "if" condition at the start of the Beginning Phase; if either is wrong then, its ability is not placed on the chain.
- **After an effect instruction:** The "if" condition is part of the effect, so the ability is placed on the chain and checks that condition when it resolves.<Rule number="383.2.b" /><Rule number="359.3.d" /><Rule number="406.5" />
  For example, <Card name="Loose Cannon" /> first instructs its controller to draw 1 and then checks hand size, so its ability is placed on the chain at the start of every Beginning Phase and checks the "if" condition during resolution.

## When do I choose whether to use a triggered ability that says "you may"? [#optional-trigger-decision]

If "you may" begins the effect, choose during finalization; if it appears later, choose during resolution.

- **Initial "you may":** Choose while the pending ability is being finalized.<Rule number="383.3.a" />
  If you decline, the ability is removed from the chain and considered not to have triggered.<Rule number="383.3.a.2" />
  If you proceed, make its required choices and pay any costs incurred during finalization before the ability becomes a finalized chain item.<Rule number="402.2" /><Rule number="404.1" /><Rule number="406.2" />
  For example, <Card name="Tideturner" /> begins its effect with "you may," so its controller decides whether to use the ability during finalization.
- **Later "you may":** If another effect instruction comes first, make the optional choice when resolution reaches that later instruction.<Rule number="383.3.a.3" />
  For example, <Card name="Ornn, Blacksmith" /> first instructs its controller to look at cards, then says "You may reveal a gear," so that choice is made during resolution.

## When do I pay costs in a triggered ability? [#costs-within-instructions]

The "[do X] to [do Y]" form creates a cost within instructions: [do X] is the cost and [do Y] is its effect.<Rule number="740.4.a" />
Pay [do X] during finalization if it begins the triggered effect or immediately follows an initial "you may."<Rule number="383.3.b" /><Rule number="383.3.b.1" /><Rule number="740.4.a.2" />
Pay it during resolution if it appears later.<Rule number="740.4.a.2.a" />

If that cost is due during finalization and you decline to pay it, the pending ability is removed from the chain without finalizing.<Rule number="404.2" />

The "[do X]. If you do, [do Y]" form does not create a cost within instructions.
The [do X] instruction is executed during resolution.<Rule number="406.5" />

For example, <Card name="Vayne, Hunter" /> says "When I conquer, you may pay <Energy value={1} /> to return me to my owner's hand."
The "pay <Energy value={1} /> to return me" form creates a cost within instructions immediately after the initial "you may," so you pay it during finalization.
If you pay, the ability finalizes and opponents may react before it resolves.<Rule number="406.4" />

By contrast, <Card name="Insightful Investigator" /> first has an opponent reveal their hand and only then offers a payment, so that cost within instructions appears later and you pay it during resolution.

## What does "play" mean on a card? [#play-definition]

"Play" has three meanings in rules text: an instruction starts the play process,<Rule number="419.1" /> a non-triggered check counts a card when it finalizes,<Rule number="419.4.b" /> and a trigger condition counts it only when it resolves.<Rule number="419.4.a" />

- **Instruction — "play a card."** This starts the play process by moving the card to the chain as a pending item.<Rule number="354" /><Rule number="354.2" />
  If a game effect gives the instruction during resolution, as on <Card name="Here to Help" /> or <Card name="Fizz, Trickster" />, the card follows every normal play step unless the effect says otherwise.<Rule number="419.3" /><Rule number="419.3.b" />
  Hiding is a separate action, so it does not satisfy an instruction to play a card.<Rule number="421.1" /><Rule number="811.1.c.1" />
- **Non-triggered check — "if you've played."** This checks whether the card finalized, not whether it resolved.<Rule number="419.4.b" />
  A finalized spell still counts if it is later countered.<Rule number="419.4.b" />
  This is the meaning used by <Card name="Battering Ram" />, <Card name="Crescent Guardian" />, <Card name="Back Off" />, and <Legion />.<Rule number="812.1.b.1" /><Rule number="812.1.c" />
- **Trigger condition — "when you play."** This waits for the card's resolution to complete.<Rule number="350.1" /><Rule number="419.4.a" />
  Units and gear resolve immediately after finalizing, but a spell resolves only after players receive priority and all pass without adding another item.<Rule number="337.2" /><Rule number="337.4" /><Rule number="339.1" />
  A countered card does not fulfill this trigger condition because it does not resolve.<Rule number="419.4.a.1" /><Rule number="425.1.b" />
  This is the meaning used by <Card name="Ravenbloom Student" /> and <Card name="Darius, Trifarian" />.

## What is the process for playing a card? [#play-process]

Playing a card moves through six play steps, then resolves according to its card category.

1. Move the card from its current zone to the chain, where it becomes a pending item waiting to be finalized.<Rule number="354" /><Rule number="354.2" />
2. Make the relevant choices, including whether to pay optional additional costs.<Rule number="355" /><Rule number="355.1.a" />
3. Determine the card's total cost.<Rule number="356" />
4. Pay that cost.<Rule number="357" />
5. Check the card's targets, costs, resulting game state, and timing permission for legality.
   If a check fails, undo the actions taken during the play process and cancel the play.<Rule number="358" /><Rule number="358.5" />
6. Finish finalizing the card.
   A unit enters the board exhausted at its chosen location, non-unit gear enters the board ready at its controller's base, and a spell becomes a finalized chain item.<Rule number="359.2.c" /><Rule number="359.2.d" /><Rule number="359.3.a" />

Units and gear resolve immediately after finalizing, without passing priority.<Rule number="337.1.a" /><Rule number="337.2" />
A finalized spell resolves only after no pending items remain and all players pass priority in sequence without adding another item.<Rule number="337.4" /><Rule number="339.1" /><Rule number="340.1" />

## When does a card leave my trash when I play it from there? [#playing-from-trash]

It leaves your trash as soon as you begin playing it, although a failed legality check undoes that move and cancels the play.
The first step of playing a card moves it from its current zone to the chain, where it waits to be finalized.<Rule number="354" /><Rule number="354.2" />
If a legality check fails, the actions taken during the play process are undone.<Rule number="358.5" />

While the play continues, the card is no longer in your trash when you make choices, determine its total cost, pay its costs, or finalize it.
It also remains outside your trash while it resolves, if it resolves.
For example, <Card name="Shadow Assassin" /> does not count itself for its "card with my name in your trash" condition; only another copy still in the trash counts.
Likewise, <Card name="Rhasa the Sunderer" /> does not include itself among the cards in your trash when determining its cost reduction.

## What makes something a target? [#target-definition]

Unless one of the exceptions below applies, a game object, player, or zone mentioned by a spell, activated ability, or triggered ability is a target.<Rule number="355.10" />
Card text does not need to say "target" or "choose"; for example, "Kill a unit" targets a unit.<Rule number="355.9.a" />

The only exceptions are:

- **Objects in non-public zones:**
  An object in a non-public zone is not a target.<Rule number="355.10.a" />
  For example, "You may play a unit from your hand" does not target a card in hand.
- **Restrictions or permissions for another choice or action:**
  Something mentioned only as a targeting restriction, or as a restriction or permission for another game action, is not a target.<Rule number="355.10.b" />
  "Kill a unit at a battlefield" targets the unit but not the battlefield, while "Play a unit from your hand to a battlefield" does not target the battlefield.
- **Objects used only in costs, trigger conditions, or replacement effects:**
  Something included only as part of a cost, trigger condition, or replacement effect is not a target.<Rule number="355.10.c" /><Rule number="355.10.c.1" />
  The dying unit in "When a friendly unit dies, kill a gear" is not a target, but the gear is.
- **Objects determined without a choice:**
  An object identified from its characteristics without that controller choosing it is not a target when no choice is ever possible.<Rule number="355.10.d" /><Rule number="355.10.d.1" />
  "Ready your legend" does not target your legend because you can have only one.
  In "Kill all units at a battlefield," the units are not targets, but the battlefield is.
  An instruction to "Kill a unit at a battlefield" still targets a unit when only one is legal.<Rule number="355.10.d.2" />
- **Sets chosen by other players:**
  An object in a set chosen wholly or partly by other players is not a target.<Rule number="355.10.e" />
  "Each player kills a unit they control" targets nothing.
- **Objects identified by "must" instructions:**
  An object identified by that instruction is not a target, even if the player chooses which object satisfies it.<Rule number="355.10.f" />
  "You must recycle one of your runes" does not target, while "Recycle a rune you control" does.

## Does "Kill a unit if it has 3 might or less" mean I can only choose a unit with 3 might or less? [#target-restrictions]

No; you can choose any otherwise legal unit, and the might condition controls only whether the kill instruction is performed.

Targeting restrictions describe the target, as in "Kill a unit with 3 might or less."<Rule number="355.9.b" />
In "Kill a unit if it has 3 might or less," the later "if" condition is instead part of the effect executed when the spell or ability resolves.<Rule number="359.3.d" /><Rule number="406.5" />

## What happens if a target becomes illegal before a spell or ability resolves? [#illegal-targets]

The spell or ability still resolves, but the illegal target is unaffected, instructions related to it cannot be followed, and independent instructions continue normally.<Rule number="359.3.e.1" /><Rule number="359.3.e.5" /><Rule number="406.5" />

If all of an instruction's targets are illegal, that instruction does not execute.<Rule number="359.3.e.7" />
If at least one target remains legal, the instruction executes using only the legal targets.<Rule number="359.3.e.8" />
If a group of targets no longer collectively meets its targeting restriction, the spell or ability's controller chooses a legal subset of the original targets for it to affect.<Rule number="355.11.b" />

A target is illegal when the spell or ability resolves if it no longer meets the targeting requirements or has changed zones to or from a non-board zone.<Rule number="359.3.e.2" />
A target that stops meeting a requirement and then meets it again is legal, but a target that changes to or from a non-board zone is a new object and remains illegal even if it returns.<Rule number="359.3.e.3" /><Rule number="359.3.e.4" />

If an instruction needs information from the illegal target, that information is null and calculations using it are ignored.<Rule number="359.3.e.12" />
If a later instruction is linked to an earlier instruction that was ignored, the later instruction is also ignored.<Rule number="359.3.e.14.a" />

## What is the difference between an ignored and a negated instruction? [#ignored-negated-instructions]

An instruction is ignored only when it isn't performed because its spell or ability mistargeted; an instruction whose effects are skipped, replaced, or prevented for another reason is negated under the August 2026 [Vendetta Rules FAQ and Clarifications](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/news/rules-and-releases/vendetta-rules-faq-and-clarifications).
Mistargeting means that a chosen target is illegal or unavailable when the instruction executes.<Rule number="359.3.e.9" />

The distinction controls later linked instructions.
If an earlier linked instruction is ignored, its later linked instructions are also ignored.<Rule number="359.3.e.14.a" />
If an earlier linked instruction is negated, later linked instructions continue unless they specifically require the negated action to have occurred.

<Callout type="warn" title="Rules citation needed">
	Core Rules 1.4 still calls instructions that fail because of circumstances other than mistargeting "ignored."<Rule number="359.3.e.6" />
	The Vendetta FAQ narrows that term to mistargeting and expressly takes precedence on this point until a later Core Rules Document says otherwise.
</Callout>

<Callout type="idea" title="Example">
	If a unit chosen by <Card name="Hidden Blade" /> is at base when the spell resolves, Hidden Blade mistargets, so its kill and linked draw instructions are ignored.
	If the unit remains a legal target but can't be killed, the kill instruction is negated instead, so its controller still draws 2.
</Callout>

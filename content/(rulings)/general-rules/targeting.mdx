## What makes something a target? [#target-definition]

A game object, player, or zone mentioned by a spell, activated ability, or triggered ability is a target unless one of the six exceptions below applies.<Rule number="355.7" /><Rule number="355.10" />

Card text does not have to use the words "target" or "choose."
For example, "Kill a unit" and "Move a friendly unit" both target the unit you select.
This also means that abilities worded as "When you choose me," such as <Card name="Irelia, Fervent" />'s ability, trigger when that object becomes a target.<Rule number="383.4.b.3" />

The six exceptions are:

- **It is in a non-public zone.**<Rule number="355.10.a" />
For example, "You may play a unit from your hand" does not target a unit because your hand is not public.
- **It only restricts another choice or game action.**<Rule number="355.10.b" />
In "Kill a unit at a battlefield," the phrase "at a battlefield" restricts which units you can target; the battlefield is not itself a target.
The battlefield is a target if the effect instead lets you choose which battlefield to affect, as in "Kill all units at a battlefield."
- **It appears only in a cost, trigger condition, or replacement effect.**<Rule number="355.10.c" /><Rule number="355.10.c.1" />
These references describe what must happen to pay a cost, trigger an ability, or apply an "instead" effect; they do not choose a target.
For example, in "When a friendly unit dies, kill a gear," the dying unit is part of the trigger condition and is not a target; only the gear is targeted.
The same applies to costs within instructions: "Kill another friendly unit to draw 1" does not target the unit killed as payment.
See [When do I pay a cost in a triggered ability that says "you may"?](/general-rules/abilities#costs-within-instructions) for more about costs within instructions.
- **The effect determines what it affects automatically.**<Rule number="355.10.d" />
In rules terms, those objects are selected by their characteristics rather than chosen by the player controlling the spell or ability.
For example, "Kill all units at battlefields" targets nothing.
What matters is whether the instruction could ever require a choice.
"Kill a unit at a battlefield" still targets a unit when there happens to be only one valid choice.<Rule number="355.10.d.1" /><Rule number="355.10.d.2" />
- **Another player helps choose the affected objects.**<Rule number="355.10.e" />
Objects in a set chosen wholly or partly by other players are not targets, even if the spell or ability's controller also chooses part of that set.
For example, "Each player kills a unit they control" targets nothing because each player chooses their unit as the effect resolves.
- **The instruction uses "must."**<Rule number="355.10.f" />
The player makes that choice while the instruction resolves rather than while the spell or ability is being played.
For example, "You must recycle one of your runes" does not target, whereas "Recycle a rune you control" does.

## Does "Kill a unit if it has 3 might or less" mean I can only choose a unit with 3 might or less? [#target-restrictions]

No.
You may choose any otherwise legal unit; the might condition does not restrict the target.

A phrase such as "with 3 might or less" describes the target, so "Kill a unit with 3 might or less" can target only a unit that meets that description.<Rule number="355.9.b" />

By contrast, "if it has 3 might or less" is a separate condition on the effect.
You check that condition when the instruction resolves.

<Callout type="warn" title="Rules citation needed">
	Rule 355.9.b explains that a target must meet its targeting restrictions, but the Core Rules do not expressly define when an "if" clause is a separate condition on an effect.
	This answer follows <Card name="Lacerate" />'s structure, which chooses a unit in one instruction and checks its might in a later instruction.
</Callout>

## What happens if a target is illegal when a spell or ability resolves? [#illegal-targets]

The spell or ability still resolves, but the illegal target is unaffected.<Rule number="359.3.e.1" /><Rule number="359.3.e.5" /><Rule number="406.5" />
Instructions involving that target are ignored, while independent instructions continue normally.<Rule number="359.3.e.5" /><Rule number="359.3.e.6" />

A target that temporarily becomes illegal is affected normally if it is legal again when the spell or ability resolves.<Rule number="359.3.e.3" />
However, a card that moves to or from a non-board zone, such as a hand or trash, is no longer the same object, even if it returns, so it remains an illegal target.<Rule number="359.3.e.4" />

If an instruction needs information from the illegal target, the rules treat that information as null and ignore calculations that use it.<Rule number="359.3.e.12" />
If a later instruction refers back to what an ignored earlier instruction did or affected, the later instruction is also ignored.
The rules call these linked instructions.<Rule number="359.3.e.14" /><Rule number="359.3.e.14.a" />

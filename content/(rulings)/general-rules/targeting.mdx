## What makes something a target? [#target-definition]

Unless one of the exceptions below applies, a game object, player, or zone mentioned by a spell, activated ability, or triggered ability is a target.<Rule number="355.10" />
Card text does not need to say "target" or "choose"; for example, "Kill a unit" targets a unit.<Rule number="355.9.a" />

The only exceptions are:

- **It is in a non-public zone.**<Rule number="355.10.a" />
  For example, "You may play a unit from your hand" does not target a card in hand.
- **It is mentioned only as a targeting restriction or as a restriction or permission for another game action.**<Rule number="355.10.b" />
  "Kill a unit at a battlefield" targets the unit but not the battlefield, while "Play a unit from your hand to a battlefield" does not target the battlefield.
- **It is included only as part of a cost, trigger condition, or replacement effect.**<Rule number="355.10.c" /><Rule number="355.10.c.1" />
  The dying unit in "When a friendly unit dies, kill a gear" is not a target, but the gear is.
  See [When do I pay a cost in a triggered ability that says "you may"?](/general-rules/abilities#costs-within-instructions) for how costs within instructions are identified and paid.
- **It is programmatically selected from its characteristics rather than chosen by the spell or ability's controller.**<Rule number="355.10.d" />
  In "Kill all units at a battlefield," the units are not targets, but the battlefield is.
  This exception applies only when no choice among those objects is ever possible.<Rule number="355.10.d.1" />
  An instruction to "Kill a unit at a battlefield" still targets a unit when only one is legal.<Rule number="355.10.d.2" />
- **It is part of a set chosen wholly or partly by other players.**<Rule number="355.10.e" />
  "Each player kills a unit they control" targets nothing.
- **It is identified by an instruction a player "must" complete.**<Rule number="355.10.f" />
  "You must recycle one of your runes" does not target, while "Recycle a rune you control" does.

## Does "Kill a unit if it has 3 might or less" mean I can only choose a unit with 3 might or less? [#target-restrictions]

No; it can target any otherwise legal unit, and the might condition controls only whether the kill instruction is performed.

Targeting restrictions describe the target, as in "Kill a unit with 3 might or less."<Rule number="355.9.b" />
In "Kill a unit if it has 3 might or less," the later "if" condition is instead part of the effect executed when the spell or ability resolves.<Rule number="359.3.d" /><Rule number="406.5" />

## What happens if a target becomes illegal before a spell or ability resolves? [#illegal-targets]

The spell or ability still resolves.<Rule number="359.3.e.1" /><Rule number="406.5" />
The illegal target is unaffected and instructions related to it cannot be followed, while independent instructions continue normally.<Rule number="359.3.e.5" /><Rule number="359.3.e.6" />

A target is illegal when the spell or ability resolves if it no longer meets the targeting requirements or has changed zones to or from a non-board zone.<Rule number="359.3.e.2" />
A target that stops meeting a requirement and then meets it again is legal, but a target that changes to or from a non-board zone is a new object and remains illegal even if it returns.<Rule number="359.3.e.3" /><Rule number="359.3.e.4" />

If an instruction needs information from the illegal target, that information is null and calculations using it are ignored.<Rule number="359.3.e.12" />
If a later instruction is linked to an earlier instruction that was ignored, the later instruction is also ignored.<Rule number="359.3.e.14.a" />

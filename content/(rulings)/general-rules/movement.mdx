## Does a unit move when it enters the board? [#entering-board]

No.
Moving means changing a permanent's position from one space on the board to another.<Rule number="448.1" />
A card entering the board from another game zone is a zone change, not a move.<Rule number="448.2" />

Playing or creating a unit at a battlefield therefore does not fulfill an ability that triggers when the unit moves there.
Replacing where a unit enters the board also does not turn that entry into a move.

## Can I choose a unit's current location as its move destination? [#chosen-destination]

No.
A destination you choose must differ from the unit's current location, but if the card specifies the destination, you make no destination choice and the unit can still be a legal target while already there.<Rule number="002" /><Rule number="355.4" /><Rule number="355.4.a" /><Rule number="355.9.a" /><Rule number="355.9.b" />
If the unit is already at that specified destination when the instruction <Term item="resolution">resolves</Term>, it cannot move because it would not change board spaces.<Rule number="448.1" />

## How are abilities triggered by exhaustion and movement ordered during one standard move? [#standard-move-trigger-sequence]

Abilities triggered by exhaustion are placed on the <Term item="chain">chain</Term> before abilities triggered by movement, but abilities that remain on the chain <Term item="resolution">resolve</Term> in the opposite order.
When several abilities trigger simultaneously, each player chooses the relative order of those they control.<Rule number="383.3.d" />
If more than one player controls them, players order their abilities starting with the turn player and proceeding in turn order.<Rule number="383.3.d.1" />

<Steps>
<Step>
A multi-unit standard move is one game action.<Rule number="144.3" />
You exhaust all the chosen units simultaneously as its cost.<Rule number="144.3.c" /><Rule number="421.3.a" />
Abilities triggered by that exhaustion trigger simultaneously before the movement occurs.<Rule number="383.2.c" /><Rule number="383.3" />
</Step>
<Step>
Although the exhaustion-triggered abilities are already on the chain, the standard move continues through its move effect because the game action executes completely.<Rule number="303.1" />
After that cost is paid, the move effect moves all the chosen units simultaneously.<Rule number="144.3" /><Rule number="203" /><Rule number="421.3.b" />
The resulting movement does not use the chain and cannot be reacted to.<Rule number="448.3.c" />
Abilities triggered by that movement trigger simultaneously and are placed after the exhaustion-triggered abilities.<Rule number="383.2.c" /><Rule number="383.3" />
</Step>
<Step>
A <Term item="cleanup">cleanup</Term> follows the move.<Rule number="455" />
Any abilities still <Term item="pending">pending</Term> wait until that cleanup and any other required tasks are completed.<Rule number="334.2" />
They are then <Term item="finalization">finalized</Term> from oldest to newest.<Rule number="337.1.b" />
</Step>
<Step>
The newest finalized ability resolves first.<Rule number="340.1" />
Among those abilities, movement-triggered abilities resolve before exhaustion-triggered abilities, and abilities triggered by the same event resolve in reverse placement order.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="idea" title="Example">
If one standard move moves <Card name="Riven, Broken Blade" />, <Card name="Seraphine, Not Alone" />, and <Card name="Stellacorn Herder" />, Riven and Seraphine trigger from becoming exhausted before Stellacorn Herder triggers from moving.
Among those three abilities, Stellacorn Herder's ability resolves first, followed by Riven's and Seraphine's abilities in the reverse of the order chosen for them.
</Callout>

## Does a later linked instruction still execute if a move is prevented or cannot happen? [#failed-move-linked-instructions]

If the move is prevented or cannot happen for a reason other than an illegal or unavailable target, yes, unless the linked instruction specifically requires the move to have occurred.<Rule number="359.3.e.14.a" /><Rule number="359.3.e.14.b" />
The failed move is negated rather than ignored, so it does not cause other linked instructions to be ignored.<Rule number="359.3.e.6" /><Rule number="359.3.e.14.a" />

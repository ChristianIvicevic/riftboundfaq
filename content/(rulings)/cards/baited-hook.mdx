## Can my opponent react before Baited Hook kills my unit? [#kill-reaction-window]

Yes.
You choose the friendly unit while finalizing the ability, as part of finishing the steps used to play it; the unit is killed later, when the ability resolves.

"Kill a friendly unit" comes after the ":" in Baited Hook's activated ability, so it's the first instruction of the effect rather than part of the cost — an activated ability's cost is everything before the ":", and its effect is everything after.<Rule number="377.1" />
Separately, that instruction selects a friendly unit and does not fall under a targeting exception.<Rule number="355.7" /><Rule number="355.10" />
The unit is therefore a target, and you choose it while finalizing the ability.<Rule number="355.8" /><Rule number="402.2" />
This is the opposite of cards like <Card name="Sacrifice" />, where "kill a friendly unit" is stated as an additional cost and is therefore excluded from targeting entirely.<Rule number="355.10.c" /><Rule number="355.10.c.1" />

After Baited Hook's ability is finalized, opponents have a normal opportunity to play Reactions before it resolves.<Rule number="377.3.b.2" /><Rule number="406.4" />
Later in the same ability, "up to 1 more than the killed unit" references the unit affected by the earlier "kill" instruction, making the two linked instructions.<Rule number="359.3.e.14" />
Because the unit has already moved to the trash by the time this later instruction checks it, the ability looks back at the unit's characteristics as they were immediately before it changed zones.<Rule number="359.3.e.13" />

<Callout type="idea" title="Example">
	You activate Baited Hook, targeting a friendly unit with 4 might.
	In response, your opponent plays <Card name="Stupefy" />, reducing that unit's might by 1 down to 3.
	Stupefy fully resolves before Baited Hook's ability does, so the unit already has 3 might at the moment it's killed.
	When the ability checks the killed unit's might, it looks back at that same reduced value.
	You may therefore banish only a unit with up to 4 might from among the top 5 cards, one lower than if Stupefy hadn't been played.
</Callout>

## What happens if the targeted unit is removed before Baited Hook's ability resolves? [#illegal-target]

The unit can't be killed, and no unit can be banished from the top 5 because Baited Hook has no killed unit's might to compare against.

If the friendly unit is no longer a legal target when the ability resolves — for example, if it was returned to its owner's hand in response — it is unaffected by the ability's "kill" instruction.<Rule number="359.3.e.2" /><Rule number="359.3.e.5" />
In rules terms, because the unit was never killed, any information the ability checks about it, including its might, returns null.<Rule number="359.3.e.12" />
"Banish a unit with might up to 1 more than the killed unit" can never be satisfied against a null might, so no unit can be chosen from among the top 5 cards, even though you still look at them.
"Then recycle the rest" is unaffected by this, since it isn't tied to the killed unit's might — you still recycle every card you looked at that wasn't banished.

## Does a unit's Deathknell resolve before or after the unit played by Baited Hook and its triggers? [#deathknell-order]

After both: the new unit enters first, and any abilities it triggers are added above the killed unit's <Deathknell />.
The <Deathknell /> can resolve only after those newer items leave the chain.

The <Deathknell /> and played unit both remain pending until Baited Hook finishes resolving.
A pending item is already on the chain but has not completed the steps needed to become finalized.<Rule number="354.3" /><Rule number="340.3" />

### Resolution Sequence [#deathknell-order-sequence]

Using <Card name="Ruined Rex" /> as an example:

1. Baited Hook starts resolving and kills Ruined Rex.
2. Ruined Rex's <Deathknell /> trigger is added to the chain as a pending item, on top of Baited Hook's still-resolving ability.<Rule number="428.1.a.1.b" /><Rule number="808.1.d.2" />
3. Baited Hook plays the selected unit from among the top 5 cards.
That unit is added to the chain as a second pending item, above the <Deathknell />.<Rule number="354.2" />
4. Baited Hook finishes resolving.
5. Ruined Rex's <Deathknell /> is the oldest pending item, so it is finalized first and you choose its target.<Rule number="337.1.b" /><Rule number="355.5" />
6. The banished unit is finalized next and resolves immediately, entering the board.<Rule number="337.2" />
7. If that unit has an ability that triggers when it is played, the ability is added to the chain above the <Deathknell />.<Rule number="383.4.a.2" /><Rule number="340.3" />
8. Once no pending items remain and players pass priority without adding to the chain, the newer triggered ability resolves first.<Rule number="337.4" /><Rule number="339.1" /><Rule number="340.1" />
9. Ruined Rex's <Deathknell /> remains on the chain, so players receive priority again.
Once they all pass without adding to the chain, the <Deathknell /> resolves.<Rule number="340.4" /><Rule number="339.1" /><Rule number="340.1" />

## If Baited Hook kills Glasc Mixologist and plays Karthus, Eternal, does Glasc Mixologist's Deathknell trigger twice? [#karthus-deathknell-timing]

No.
<Card name="Karthus, Eternal" /> makes your <Deathknell /> effects trigger an additional time, but he is not on the board when Glasc Mixologist dies, so Glasc's <Deathknell /> triggers only once.

When Baited Hook kills <Card name="Glasc Mixologist" />, Glasc's <Deathknell /> is added to the chain as a pending item before he is moved to the trash.<Rule number="428.1.a.1.b" /><Rule number="808.1.d.2" />
A pending item waits on the chain to finish being played while Baited Hook continues resolving.
Baited Hook then continues resolving and may banish Karthus from among the top 5 cards and play him, placing him on the chain above Glasc's <Deathknell />.<Rule number="354.2" />

After Baited Hook finishes, Glasc's <Deathknell /> is finalized first.<Rule number="337.1.b" />
Karthus is finalized next and immediately enters the board before the <Deathknell /> can resolve.<Rule number="337.2" />
Karthus's passive ability is only active while he is on the board.<Rule number="365.1" /><Rule number="366.1" />
It was not active when Glasc died, and Karthus entering later cannot cause that earlier death to trigger Glasc's <Deathknell /> again.
Glasc's <Deathknell /> resolves once, so it can play only one unit from the trash.

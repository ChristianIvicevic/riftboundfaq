## How does Switcheroo work? [#might-swap]

Switcheroo reverses the units' current might when it <Term item="resolution">resolves</Term>, but it does not exchange their base might or set either unit's might to a new value.
The Swap action determines the difference between the two current values, then creates a might increase for the lower value and an equal might decrease for the higher value.<Rule number="434.1" /><Rule number="434.1.a" /><Rule number="434.1.b" />
Each increase or decrease lasts for Switcheroo's stated duration, this turn.<Rule number="434.1.a" />
If the units have equal might when Switcheroo resolves, the Swap has no effect.<Rule number="434.1.c" />

## Are existing equipment bonuses or passive effects counted again after Switcheroo resolves? [#existing-modifiers]

No, each existing modifier is counted once as part of the unit's current might, and it continues applying normally after Switcheroo <Term item="resolution">resolves</Term>.<Rule number="478.2" /><Rule number="479.4.a" /><Rule number="479.4.d" />
Switcheroo calculates one difference from the complete current values and creates separate increase and decrease effects; it does not remove, reapply, or replace the existing modifiers.<Rule number="434.1.a" /><Rule number="434.1.b" />
For example, if a unit has a base might of 2<Might /> and an active +3<Might /> bonus, its current might is 5<Might /> for the Swap calculation.
After the Swap, the +3<Might /> remains one part of that unit's might rather than being added a second time.

## What happens when equipment detaches or passives stop applying after Switcheroo? [#later-modifier-changes]

Only the modifier that stops applying changes the unit's might.<Rule number="478.4" /><Rule number="479.4.a" />
Switcheroo's increase or decrease keeps its originally calculated amount until end of turn.
The Swap creates effects with fixed amounts when it <Term item="resolution">resolves</Term>, so later changes do not cause it to recalculate the difference.<Rule number="434.1.a" /><Rule number="434.1.b" />

<Callout type="idea" title="Example">
	<Card name="Wuju Bladesman - Starter" /> grants a friendly unit +2<Might /> while it defends alone.
	Suppose your <Card name="Pakaa Cub" /> has 3<Might /> before that bonus and defends alone, while an opposing <Card name="Determined Sentry" /> attacks with 1<Might />.
	The bonus from Wuju Bladesman raises Pakaa Cub to 5<Might />.
	Your opponent plays Switcheroo targeting both units.
	Switcheroo gives Pakaa Cub -4<Might /> and Determined Sentry +4<Might />, leaving them at 1<Might /> and 5<Might />, respectively.
	If Pakaa Cub stops defending alone, it loses that +2<Might /> bonus but keeps Switcheroo's -4<Might /> decrease, leaving it at -1<Might />.
</Callout>

## Can Switcheroo result in negative might? [#negative-might]

Yes, a unit can have negative might if a bonus included in Switcheroo's calculation stops applying while Switcheroo's decrease remains.<Rule number="434.1.a" /><Rule number="434.1.b" /><Rule number="478.4" />
Spells, abilities, and combat-damage assignment treat negative might as 0<Might />.<Rule number="143.2.c" />
Might increases and decreases instead use the actual negative value.<Rule number="143.2.c.1" />
The unit does not die merely for having 0<Might /> or negative might.
At <Term item="cleanup">cleanup</Term>, it is killed only if it has a non-zero amount of marked damage equal to or greater than its might.<Rule number="142.4.b" /><Rule number="323.4" />

<Callout type="idea" title="Example">
	A <Card name="Scuttle Crab" /> at -2<Might /> is targeted with <Card name="Punch First" />, which reads "Give a unit +5<Might /> this turn."
	Scuttle Crab ends up with 3<Might /> because the +5<Might /> increase uses -2<Might />, not 0<Might />.
</Callout>

## How does Elder Dragon affect combat damage assignment? [#combat-damage-assignment]

While you control Elder Dragon on the board, 1 combat damage from your units is lethal to an enemy unit regardless of its might, unless another effect changes how much must be assigned or prevents that damage.
Elder Dragon changes the lethal-damage value for enemy units with its controller's damage, and the smallest valid amount of damage is 1.<Rule number="142.3.b" /><Rule number="142.4.c" /><Rule number="418.1.e" />

You must assign lethal damage to one unit before assigning damage to another, and you cannot assign more than the lethal minimum while another unit remains.<Rule number="467.2.c.3" /><Rule number="467.2.c.4" />
With Elder Dragon, that normally means assigning exactly 1 damage to an enemy unit before moving to the next.
Passive abilities and replacement effects, including prevention, are considered during assignment and can change this minimum.<Rule number="467.2.c.4.a" /><Rule number="467.2.c.5" />

<Callout type="idea" title="Elder Dragon vs. Imperial Decree">
	Suppose <Card name="Pakaa Cub" />, with 3 might, is your only attacker against three undamaged copies of <Card name="Noxus Hopeful" />, each defending with 4 might.
	With Elder Dragon, you assign 1 damage from Pakaa Cub to each Noxus Hopeful, and all three are killed during the combat <Term item="cleanup">cleanup</Term>.<Rule number="323.4" /><Rule number="467.2.d" /><Rule number="468.1.a" />

	<Card name="Imperial Decree" /> does not change lethal damage or combat damage assignment.
	Assigning damage is not dealing damage, so its delayed triggered ability triggers only after a unit actually takes damage and then attempts to kill that unit.<Rule number="383.2.c" /><Rule number="391.2" /><Rule number="467.2.c.1" />

	With Imperial Decree instead, Pakaa Cub still assigns all 3 damage, but you cannot split it among the three Noxus Hopefuls.
	You must assign lethal damage to one before moving to another, so all 3 damage is assigned to the Noxus Hopeful you choose first.
	Of the three Noxus Hopefuls, only that one takes Pakaa Cub's combat damage, so Imperial Decree's kill ability triggers for it and not the other two.
</Callout>

## Does damage from earlier in the turn become lethal when Elder Dragon enters, and can I react? [#lethal-damage-timing]

Damage still marked by Elder Dragon's controller becomes lethal to enemy units when Elder Dragon enters, and opponents cannot react after it enters before the resulting <Term item="cleanup">cleanup</Term> handles that lethal damage.
Elder Dragon's "your damage" means damage marked by its controller.<Rule number="142.3.b" />
Its first ability is a passive statement that changes the lethal-damage value for those enemy units while Elder Dragon is on the board.<Rule number="142.4.c" /><Rule number="364.2" /><Rule number="365.1" />

<Term item="finalization">Finalizing</Term> a unit does not pass <Term item="priority">priority</Term>, and the unit <Term item="resolution">resolves</Term> immediately.<Rule number="337.1.a" /><Rule number="337.2" />
After Elder Dragon enters, a cleanup happens without passing or awarding priority, and every unit with lethal damage is killed unless that death is replaced.<Rule number="319.6" /><Rule number="320.1" /><Rule number="323.4" /><Rule number="370.1" /><Rule number="370.1.c" />
Elder Dragon's separate "When you play me" ability is a triggered play effect, but it does not create a response window before this lethal-damage check.<Rule number="383.4.a" /><Rule number="383.4.a.2" />

<Callout type="idea" title="Example">
	You play <Card name="Flurry of Blades" /> during your turn, and its 1 damage remains marked on enemy units at battlefields.
	When you later play Elder Dragon directly, each of those units is killed in the cleanup caused by Elder Dragon entering before your opponents can react.

	The same result occurs if <Card name="Dazzling Aurora" /> plays Elder Dragon at the end of your turn.
	End-of-turn abilities happen in the Ending Step, before the Expiration Step's special cleanup heals all units, so reaching the end of the turn has not itself healed Flurry of Blades' damage yet.<Rule number="317.1.a" /><Rule number="317.2.a" /><Rule number="317.2.b" />
	Opponents may react while Dazzling Aurora's triggered ability waits to resolve.<Rule number="383.1" /><Rule number="337.4" /><Rule number="338.1.a.2" />
	Once that ability begins resolving and reveals Elder Dragon, no new priority window opens before Elder Dragon enters and the affected units are killed.
</Callout>

## Can Elder Dragon target a unit after Zhonya's Hourglass recalls it? [#zhonyas-target-timing]

Yes, if you play Elder Dragon while an enemy unit at a battlefield has your damage marked on it and that unit's controller controls <Card name="Zhonya's Hourglass" /> in their base: Zhonya's Hourglass replaces that unit's death and recalls it before you choose targets for Elder Dragon's play effect, so you can choose the recalled unit in its controller's base.
If another valid enemy unit remains at a battlefield, you can choose that unit as well because it is at a different location.

<Steps>
<Step>
Elder Dragon enters, and its passive makes your marked damage lethal to the damaged enemy unit.<Rule number="142.4.c" />
Its play effect is then added to the <Term item="chain">chain</Term> as a <Term item="pending">pending</Term> item.<Rule number="383.4.a.2" />
</Step>
<Step>
Both Elder Dragon entering and its play effect being added require <Term item="cleanup">cleanup</Term> before play continues.<Rule number="319.6" /><Rule number="319.3" />
All outstanding cleanups finish before the pending effect can begin <Term item="finalization">finalization</Term>.<Rule number="320" /><Rule number="334.2" />
</Step>
<Step>
During cleanup, the damaged unit would be killed.<Rule number="323.4" />
Zhonya's Hourglass replaces that death: the Hourglass is killed instead, and the unit is healed, exhausted, and recalled to its controller's base rather than killed.<Rule number="370.1.a.1" />
</Step>
<Step>
Killing Zhonya's Hourglass sends it from the board to the trash, making another cleanup outstanding after the current one.<Rule number="429.1" /><Rule number="319.6" /><Rule number="322" />
</Step>
<Step>
After all cleanups finish, you choose targets while finalizing the play effect.<Rule number="334.2" /><Rule number="337.1" /><Rule number="191.5.c" /><Rule number="403.2" />
The recalled unit is now in base, and any valid enemy unit that remains at a battlefield is at a different location, so both can be chosen.<Rule number="146.1" /><Rule number="198.1" /><Rule number="355.11.a" />
</Step>
</Steps>

When the effect <Term item="resolution">resolves</Term>, it deals 1 to each target it affects, marking damage on each unit separately.<Rule number="418.1.b" /><Rule number="418.1.c" />
If Elder Dragon remains on the board, its passive makes that damage lethal, so each affected unit would be killed during the cleanup after the ability leaves the chain.<Rule number="142.3.b" /><Rule number="142.4.c" /><Rule number="365.1" /><Rule number="407.5" /><Rule number="319.5" /><Rule number="323.4" />

## Does Elder Dragon's play effect finalize if its passive leaves no enemy units to target? [#zero-target-finalization]

Yes.
The words "up to" allow Elder Dragon's controller to choose zero targets.<Rule number="355.13" />
Because the ability does not begin with "you may," its controller cannot decline to perform it, so the play effect <Term item="finalization">finalizes</Term> even when no enemy units remain.<Rule number="383.3.a" /><Rule number="403.4" /><Rule number="407.2" />

After Elder Dragon enters, its play effect is added to the <Term item="chain">chain</Term> before the resulting <Term item="cleanup">cleanup</Term>.<Rule number="319.6" /><Rule number="334.2.a" /><Rule number="383.4.a.2" />
Elder Dragon's passive ability makes its controller's marked damage on enemy units lethal, so that cleanup may kill an opponent's last units.<Rule number="142.4.c" /><Rule number="323.4" />
The <Term item="pending">pending</Term> play effect remains on the chain throughout the cleanup, so the opponent retains control of their empty battlefields and their <Hidden /> cards remain there.<Rule number="320" /><Rule number="323.5" /><Rule number="323.6.a" /><Rule number="330" /><Rule number="331.1" />
After the play effect finalizes with zero targets, the opponent may play an eligible card from face down as a <Reaction /> before the effect <Term item="resolution">resolves</Term>.<Rule number="407.4" />
When the effect resolves with no targets, it simply does nothing.

## When do I choose whether to make Rebuttal's optional payment? [#power-payment-timing]

You choose the affected spell during Rebuttal's <Term item="finalization">finalization</Term>, before opponents can react.<Rule number="355.5" /><Rule number="359.3.c" />
You do not decide whether to pay <Universal /> until Rebuttal <Term item="resolution">resolves</Term>.
The optional payment is not a cost paid to play Rebuttal; it is a game action Rebuttal instructs you to perform during resolution.<Rule number="135.2.b.3" /><Rule number="205" />
Opponents therefore know which spell you chose, but not whether you will pay to gain control of it or decline and have Rebuttal attempt to counter it.

## What choices can I remake when Rebuttal takes control of a spell? [#new-choices]

You may decline to make new choices and leave every existing choice unchanged, or remake every eligible choice the spell made.<Rule number="753" />
The eligible categories are play locations, modes, move destinations, and targets, all of which are choices normally made during <Term item="finalization">finalization</Term>.<Rule number="752" /><Rule number="752.1" />
Choices made "as you play this" and choices about optional additional costs cannot be remade.<Rule number="752.2" />

If you make new choices, every resulting choice must be legal.<Rule number="753.1" />
You cannot choose to make new choices unless a complete legal set is available.<Rule number="753.2" />
You may select the same option again, but it still counts as remaking that choice.<Rule number="753" />
If the spell targets a game object after you remake its choices, that object's applicable targeting effects trigger, even if the spell targeted it before.<Rule number="754" />

If you decline, the existing choices remain unchanged even if a target has become illegal.<Rule number="355.15" />
The spell still resolves, but an illegal target is unaffected.<Rule number="359.3.e.1" /><Rule number="359.3.e.5" />

## Can Rebuttal redirect a spell played from face down to another location? [#hidden-target-restriction]

No.
Restrictions already applied to a spell, including the targeting restriction imposed by <Hidden />, also apply to its new choices.<Rule number="753.3" />

Rebuttal allows you to remake a <Term item="finalization">finalized</Term> spell's targets,<Rule number="752.1" /> but every new target must still be legal.<Rule number="753.1" />
When a spell is played from face down, its targets must be chosen from among the options at the battlefield where it was hidden unless the spell's own targeting restriction makes that impossible.<Rule number="811.1.d.2" />
Changing the spell's controller does not change where it was played from, so this restriction continues to apply.

<Callout type="idea" title="Example">
	An opponent plays <Card name="Hidden Blade" /> from face down and chooses a unit at that battlefield.
	If you gain control of Hidden Blade with Rebuttal, you may choose another legal unit at the same battlefield.
	You cannot choose a unit at another battlefield, and you cannot choose a unit at base because Hidden Blade itself requires a unit "at a battlefield."
</Callout>

## Whose "when you play a spell" abilities trigger for a spell Rebuttal takes control of? [#play-trigger-controller]

Under the August 2026 [Vendetta FAQ](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/news/rules-and-releases/vendetta-rules-faq-and-clarifications), if the spell <Term item="resolution">resolves</Term> after Rebuttal takes control of it, the spell's new controller's applicable "when you play a spell" abilities trigger; the original player's abilities do not.
These abilities trigger once the spell has finished resolving.<Rule number="420.4.a" />
Rebuttal has made the player who gained control of the spell its controller by then.

<Callout type="warn" title="Rules citation needed">
	Core Rules 1.5 says an ability that triggers when "you" do something checks for a game action for which that player is responsible.<Rule number="412.4" />
	It does not say that gaining control of a finalized spell transfers responsibility for the spell's earlier play game action.
	The new-controller outcome above therefore still relies on the official FAQ ruling.
</Callout>

<Callout type="idea" title="Example">
	Suppose you and your opponent each control <Card name="Ravenbloom Student" />, and you use Rebuttal to gain control of your opponent's <Card name="Gust" />.
	Your Ravenbloom Student triggers when Rebuttal resolves under your control.
	If Gust then resolves under your control, your Ravenbloom Student triggers again, while your opponent's Ravenbloom Student does not trigger for Gust.
</Callout>

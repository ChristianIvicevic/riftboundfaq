## When do I choose whether to pay the power for Rebuttal? [#power-payment-timing]

You choose whether to pay <Universal /> when Rebuttal resolves because the optional payment is a cost within the spell's instructions, not an additional cost to play it.

When Rebuttal is finalized, you choose the spell with energy cost no more than <Energy value={4} />.<Rule number="355.5" />
The optional power payment is not an additional cost to play Rebuttal; it is a cost within the spell's instructions.<Rule number="204.2" /><Rule number="204.3" />
Costs within a spell's instructions are paid during that spell's resolution.<Rule number="204.3.b" />

Opponents can therefore react to Rebuttal knowing which spell it chose, but without knowing whether you will pay <Universal /> to gain control of that spell or decline to pay and counter it.

## Can Rebuttal redirect a spell played from Hidden to another location? [#hidden-target-restriction]

No — taking control of the spell and making new choices for it does not remove the targeting restriction imposed by <Hidden />.

Rebuttal allows its controller to remake a finalized spell's targets,<Rule number="752.1" /> but every new choice must still be legal.<Rule number="753.1" />
When a spell is played from <Hidden />, its targets must be chosen from among the options at the battlefield where it was hidden unless the spell's own targeting restriction makes that impossible.<Rule number="811.1.d.2" />
Changing the spell's controller does not change where it was played from, so this restriction continues to apply.

<Callout type="idea" title="Example">
	An opponent plays <Card name="Hidden Blade" /> from <Hidden /> and chooses a unit at that battlefield.
	If you gain control of Hidden Blade with Rebuttal, you may choose another legal unit at the same battlefield.
	You cannot choose a unit at another battlefield, and you cannot choose a unit at base because Hidden Blade itself requires a unit "at a battlefield."
</Callout>

## Whose "when you play a spell" abilities trigger for a spell Rebuttal takes control of? [#play-trigger-controller]

The abilities controlled by the spell's new controller trigger because the act of playing the spell is completed when it resolves under that player's control.

Abilities such as <Card name="Ravenbloom Student" />'s do not trigger when a spell is first placed on or finalized to the chain.
They trigger when the act of playing that spell is completed by its resolution.<Rule number="419.4.a" />
At that point, the player who gained control of the spell with Rebuttal is its controller.<Rule number="191.2" />

<Callout type="idea" title="Example">
	You control Ravenbloom Student and use Rebuttal to gain control of an opponent's <Card name="Gust" />.
	Your Student triggers when Rebuttal resolves.
	When Gust resolves afterward, your Student triggers again because you now control Gust.
	Your opponent's "when you play a spell" abilities do not trigger for Gust.
</Callout>

## Do I draw a card if I take control of Back Off? [#back-off-draw]

No — you did not play <Card name="Back Off" /> from your hand merely by taking control of it.

To play a card, a player places it on the chain and queues it for finalization.<Rule number="419.1" />
Back Off's "if you played this from your hand" clause is a non-triggered condition, so it checks whether the card was finalized by that player.<Rule number="419.4.b" /><Rule number="329.3" />
Gaining control of an already-finalized spell does not repeat that process or change which player performed it.

When Back Off resolves under your control, "you" refers to you, but you did not play it from your hand, so you do not draw a card.
The opponent who originally played Back Off does not draw either because they no longer control the resolving spell.

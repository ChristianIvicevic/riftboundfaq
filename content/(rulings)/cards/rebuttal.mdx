## When do I choose whether to pay the power for Rebuttal? [#power-payment-timing]

You choose whether to pay <Universal /> when Rebuttal resolves.
The optional payment is a game action, not a cost, because its effect appears in a separate conditional instruction.<Rule number="205" />
Because the payment instruction states no other timing, it executes during resolution.<Rule number="135.2.b.3" />

During Rebuttal's finalization, you choose the spell with energy cost no more than <Energy value={4} />.<Rule number="355.5" />
Opponents can therefore react to Rebuttal knowing which spell it chose, but without knowing whether you will pay <Universal /> to gain control of that spell or decline to pay and counter it.<Rule number="359.3.c" />

## Can Rebuttal redirect a spell played from Hidden to another location? [#hidden-target-restriction]

No.
Taking control of the spell and making new choices for it does not remove the targeting restriction imposed by <Hidden />.

Rebuttal allows you to remake a finalized spell's targets,<Rule number="752.1" /> but every new choice must still be legal.<Rule number="753.1" />
When a spell is played from <Hidden />, its targets must be chosen from among the options at the battlefield where it was hidden unless the spell's own targeting restriction makes that impossible.<Rule number="811.1.d.2" />
Changing the spell's controller does not change where it was played from, so this restriction continues to apply.

<Callout type="idea" title="Example">
	An opponent plays <Card name="Hidden Blade" /> from <Hidden /> and chooses a unit at that battlefield.
	If you gain control of Hidden Blade with Rebuttal, you may choose another legal unit at the same battlefield.
	You cannot choose a unit at another battlefield, and you cannot choose a unit at base because Hidden Blade itself requires a unit "at a battlefield."
</Callout>

## Whose "when you play a spell" abilities trigger for a spell Rebuttal takes control of? [#play-trigger-controller]

If the spell resolves after Rebuttal takes control of it, the spell's new controller's applicable "when you play a spell" abilities trigger; the original player's abilities do not.
These abilities trigger when the spell's Play is completed by its resolution.<Rule number="419.4.a" />
At that point, the player who gained control of the spell with Rebuttal is its controller.<Rule number="191.2" />

See [In what order are multiple pending chain items finalized and resolved?](/general-rules/chain-and-priority#pending-item-order) for the general ordering rules.

### Resolution Sequence [#play-trigger-controller-sequence]

Suppose you and your opponent each control <Card name="Ravenbloom Student" />, and you use Rebuttal to gain control of your opponent's <Card name="Gust" />.

1. Your opponent plays Gust, which becomes a finalized chain item after its choices and costs are handled.<Rule number="419.1" /><Rule number="359.3.a" />
2. You play Rebuttal and choose Gust while Rebuttal is pending during finalization.<Rule number="354.2" /><Rule number="355.5" />
   Rebuttal then becomes the newer finalized item above Gust.<Rule number="359.3.a" />
3. After all players pass priority without adding another item, Rebuttal resolves first because it is newer than Gust.<Rule number="339.1" /><Rule number="340.1" />
4. You pay <Universal />, gain control of Gust, and may make new legal choices for it while Rebuttal resolves.<Rule number="752.1" /><Rule number="753.1" />
5. When Rebuttal finishes resolving, your Ravenbloom Student ability is added to the chain as a pending item because you control Rebuttal when its Play is completed.<Rule number="383.2.c" /><Rule number="383.3" /><Rule number="329" /><Rule number="191.2" /><Rule number="419.4.a" />
6. The chain returns to finalization, so the pending Ravenbloom Student ability finalizes before priority is granted.<Rule number="340.3" /><Rule number="337.1" /><Rule number="337.4" />
   It then resolves before Gust after all players pass.<Rule number="339.1" /><Rule number="340.1" />
7. Players receive priority again before Gust resolves and may add new <Reaction /> cards or abilities.<Rule number="340.4" /><Rule number="338.1.a" /><Rule number="338.1.a.2" />
8. If Gust then resolves, your Ravenbloom Student triggers again because you control Gust when its Play is completed; your opponent's Ravenbloom Student does not trigger for Gust.<Rule number="191.2" /><Rule number="419.4.a" />

See [Can I play a reaction after one, but not all, items on the chain have resolved?](/general-rules/chain-and-priority#partial-chain-reactions) for the general priority procedure between resolutions.

## Do I draw a card if I take control of Back Off? [#back-off-draw]

No.
Taking control of <Card name="Back Off" /> does not make you responsible for playing it from hand.

The opponent who placed Back Off on the chain and queued it for finalization performed its Play action and remains responsible for it.<Rule number="419.1" /><Rule number="411.1" />
Gaining control of the already-finalized spell does not restart that process.
Back Off's "if you played this from your hand" clause is a non-triggered condition, so it counts the card as played once it was finalized.<Rule number="419.4.b" /><Rule number="329.3" />

Back Off resolves under your control, but you did not play it from hand, so its draw instruction does not apply to you.<Rule number="191.2" /><Rule number="359.3.d" />
The opponent who played Back Off does not draw either because they no longer control the resolving spell.

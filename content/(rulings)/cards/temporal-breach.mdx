## Can Temporal Breach banish a unit at a different battlefield if played from Hidden? [#hidden-targeting-restriction]

No, when Temporal Breach is played from Hidden, the unit it banishes must be chosen from among units at the battlefield where it was hidden.

"Banish a unit" chooses a specific game object to affect, making it a target, declared as Temporal Breach is put on the chain.<Rule number="355.5" /><Rule number="355.7" /><Rule number="355.8" />
Choices made while playing a card from Hidden are restricted to the battlefield where it was hidden.<Rule number="811.1.d" />
Specifically, if a Hidden spell chooses any targets, those targets must be chosen from among options at that battlefield, unless the ability explicitly restricts targeting in a way that makes this impossible.<Rule number="811.1.d.2" />
Temporal Breach's own wording doesn't restrict its target to a location, so this Hidden restriction applies in full — a unit at another battlefield, or in base, isn't a legal target when it's played from Hidden.

If Temporal Breach is played normally, the Hidden restriction does not apply.
It may target an otherwise legal unit at any board location, including a base or battlefield.<Rule number="811.3" />

## Does a unit replayed by Temporal Breach keep temporary modifications or statuses? [#replayed-unit-state]

No, the replayed unit does not keep temporary modifications or statuses it had before Temporal Breach banished it.

Banishment is a non-board zone,<Rule number="056.1" /> and a game object that changes zones to or from a non-board zone becomes a new object for tracking purposes.<Rule number="124" />
When the unit enters banishment, all temporary modifications and statuses on it are cleared.<Rule number="124.1" />
This means effects such as <Card name="Stupefy" />'s might reduction no longer apply, and a unit that was <Empowered /> is no longer <Empowered /> when it is replayed.<Rule number="124.2" />

## What happens to equipment attached to a unit replayed by Temporal Breach? [#attached-equipment]

The equipment detaches and remains at the unit's previous location because Temporal Breach moves the unit from the board to banishment.

When Temporal Breach moves the unit from the board to banishment, all cards attached to that unit detach and remain at the unit's previous location.<Rule number="719.5" /><Rule number="435.4.b" />
The replayed unit is a new game object,<Rule number="124" /> and nothing instructs the equipment to attach to it again.

If the unit was at a battlefield, the unattached equipment is recalled to its controller's base during the next cleanup.<Rule number="435.4.a" />
If the unit was at a base, the equipment instead remains unattached at that base.

## Does a replayed unit trigger its attack or defend ability again during the same combat? [#replayed-combat-triggers]

Yes, if the unit is replayed to the battlefield where combat is in progress, its attack or defend ability can trigger again because it becomes a new game object.

The unit becomes a new game object when it enters banishment,<Rule number="124" /> and that new object has not yet had its attack or defend trigger condition checked during that combat.
After the replayed unit enters the battlefield, the next cleanup gives it the same attacker or defender designation as its controller.<Rule number="323.2.a" />
Gaining that designation causes its corresponding attack or defend ability to trigger.<Rule number="383.4.e" /><Rule number="383.4.f" />
The normal once-per-combat limit does not prevent this trigger because it applied to the previous game object, not the newly replayed one.<Rule number="383.4.e.2.a" /><Rule number="383.4.f.2.a" />

<Callout type="idea" title="Example">
  <Card name="Ahri, Inquisitive" />'s "When I attack or defend" ability can trigger when she gains the defender designation at the start of combat.
  After that ability resolves, Temporal Breach can banish and replay her to the same battlefield.
  The replayed Ahri is a new game object and gains the defender designation during the next cleanup, triggering her ability a second time in the same combat.
</Callout>

## Does Ravenbloom Conservatory trigger again if Temporal Breach replays my only defending unit? [#ravenbloom-conservatory]

No, <Card name="Ravenbloom Conservatory" /> does not trigger a second time because it remains the same game object, and its defend trigger has already checked its condition during that combat.

Ravenbloom Conservatory's "When you defend here" ability is a defend trigger whose condition is fulfilled when its controller gains the defender designation.<Rule number="383.4.f" />
When combat opens, the defending player and their units separately gain that designation,<Rule number="464.2.c.3" /> so Ravenbloom Conservatory checks its trigger condition then.
A defend triggered ability can have its condition checked only once per combat.<Rule number="383.4.f.2.a" />

Temporal Breach makes the replayed unit a new game object,<Rule number="124" /> which allows that new unit's own defend ability to receive its first condition check during the same combat.
It does not make Ravenbloom Conservatory a new object or begin a new combat, so Ravenbloom Conservatory's ability does not receive another condition check.
The defending player also keeps the defender designation until combat ends,<Rule number="466.7.a" /> and therefore does not gain it again when the replayed unit gains its separate designation during cleanup.

## What happens if Temporal Breach banishes a token unit? [#token-unit]

The token stops existing the moment it's banished, so there's nothing left for its owner to replay.

Temporal Breach puts the token into its owner's banishment, which is a non-board zone.<Rule number="056.1" />
Tokens can only exist on the board or the chain — if a token enters any other zone, it ceases to exist immediately upon arriving there.<Rule number="186" /><Rule number="186.1" />
So by the time Temporal Breach's "then its owner plays it to the same location" instruction would apply, the token is already gone, and that part of the spell does nothing for it.<Rule number="055" />

## Can I pay optional additional costs, like Accelerate, when replaying a unit banished by Temporal Breach? [#optional-additional-costs]

Yes, since the replayed unit still goes through the complete, normal process of playing a card, and "ignoring its cost" only narrows one part of that process — the base cost — leaving everything else, including optional additional costs, unaffected.

"Its owner plays it" instructs the normal process of playing a card — the unit is removed from banishment and put onto the chain as a pending item,<Rule number="354" /><Rule number="354.2" /> it isn't placed directly onto the board.
That pending item goes through the full finalization process once it's ready to be finalized, including making relevant choices<Rule number="355" /> and determining total cost.<Rule number="356" />
Playing a card "ignoring its cost" only sets its base Energy and base Power cost to zero;<Rule number="356.1.b.1" /> further additional costs applied in later steps can still raise the total cost back above zero.<Rule number="356.1.b.3" />
<Accelerate /> is one such optional additional cost, paid as a player plays the unit with the ability,<Rule number="805.2" /> so its owner may still declare their intent to pay it during finalization<Rule number="356.2.b.1" /> and pay it as usual, unaffected by Temporal Breach ignoring the unit's base cost.

## If the unit banished by Temporal Breach has Hidden, can its owner hide it instead of playing it? [#hide-instead-of-play]

No, hiding the unit does not fulfill Temporal Breach's instruction to play it.

Hide is a separate action, not a subset of play.<Rule number="811.1.c.1" />
Playing the unit puts it on the chain and queues it for finalization,<Rule number="419.1" /> whereas hiding would put it facedown at a battlefield.<Rule number="421.1" />
Moreover, the unit is in banishment when its owner is instructed to play it, and <Hidden /> only permits a card to be hidden from hand or the champion zone.<Rule number="811.1.b" />
Hiding is therefore not an alternative to the instructed play.

## If Temporal Breach banishes and replays my only unit at a battlefield, can I score there on my opponent's turn? [#score-on-opponents-turn]

No, as control of the battlefield is never lost during this sequence.

When Temporal Breach resolves, the replayed unit is added to the chain as a pending item,<Rule number="354.2" /> with its play process held until the spell has fully resolved.<Rule number="354.3" />
Once the spell finishes resolving, a cleanup runs — but the pending unit keeps the chain alive throughout it.<Rule number="330" /><Rule number="331" />
Because the chain is not empty, the turn remains in a closed state, and cleanup step 4 — which would make the battlefield uncontrolled — only applies during an open state.<Rule number="190.4.c" /><Rule number="323.6" />
Control is never lost, so there is no opportunity to score a point.<Rule number="469.1" />
When all outstanding tasks are complete, the pending unit is finalized<Rule number="334.2" /><Rule number="337.1" /> and replayed to the same battlefield you still control, as dictated by Temporal Breach's own text.<Rule number="359.2" />

## What happens if Temporal Breach banishes a unit at Rockfall Path? [#rockfall-path]

Temporal Breach banishes the unit, but its owner cannot play it back to <Card name="Rockfall Path" />.
The unit remains in its owner's banishment.

Rockfall Path prevents the instructed play action.
This does not stop Temporal Breach from resolving; when it reaches the replay instruction, that game action is skipped as impossible.<Rule number="358.3.a" />
The earlier banish instruction is unaffected, so the unit remains banished.

## What happens if Brynhir Thundersong prevents the owner of a unit banished by Temporal Breach from playing cards? [#brynhir-thundersong]

The unit remains banished.

If <Card name="Brynhir Thundersong" />'s play effect has resolved, opponents cannot play cards for the rest of that turn.
Temporal Breach can still banish an opponent's unit, but that opponent cannot perform the instruction to play it back.

This does not stop Temporal Breach from resolving.
The replay is an impossible game action and is skipped,<Rule number="358.3.a" /> just as it is when Rockfall Path prevents the unit from being played to its previous location.
Because the earlier banish instruction was completed normally, the unit remains in its owner's banishment.
